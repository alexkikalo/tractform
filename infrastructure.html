Water & Power

The systems everything else depends on

Select a topic for demand, options, and practical constraints.

Water

Source, yield, storage, and distribution — matched to household, animals, and irrigation.

Demand

People: ~50–100+ gal/day/person depending on lifestyle. Livestock and irrigation often dwarf household use. Size for peak, not average.

Well

Depth, yield (gpm), and water quality. A high-yielding shallow well can be better than a deep weak one — test and document.

Storage

Tanks buy time during outages and low yield. Gravity feed from elevation reduces pump runtime when possible.

Other sources

Spring development, rain catchment, surface water — each has legal and reliability limits. Know rights before you depend on them.

Power

Grid, solar, generator, or hybrid — driven by load, distance to line, and independence goals.

Loads

List pumps, tools, refrigeration, heat, and lights. Peak and continuous loads decide system size more than wishful averages.

Grid extension

Cost scales with distance and terrain. Get a utility estimate early; it often changes the solar vs grid decision.

Solar + battery

Panel array sized to daily kWh; batteries sized to nights and cloudy stretches. Generator backup is common for reality, not failure.

Generator

Useful for peak and backup. Fuel storage, noise, and maintenance are part of the system — not afterthoughts.

Septic

Soil and household size drive system type more than preference.

Perc & soil

Percolation tests and soil depth determine conventional drain field vs mound or alternative systems.

Sizing

Bedroom count and occupancy set tank and field size under most codes. Plan for guests and growth honestly.

Placement

Setbacks from wells, property lines, and buildings. Don’t paint yourself into a corner with the first structure.

Maintenance

Pumping schedule and what not to put down the drain. Neglect is the usual failure mode, not age alone.
