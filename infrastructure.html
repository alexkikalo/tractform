Stage 2 of the path

Infrastructure

Water and power first. These are the systems that make buildings, animals, gardens, and daily life possible. Get them right and everything downstream becomes simpler.

The question this stage answers

“What water and power systems does this land need to support the life I want — and what will it cost and take to put them in place?”

Inputs

What you tell Tractform about the land and your needs.

1 Existing conditions

  • • Current water sources (well, spring, creek, pond, municipal, none)
  • • Well depth, yield, and water quality if known
  • • Existing power (grid, solar, generator, none)
  • • Distance to nearest power line or transformer
  • • Existing septic or sewer connection
  • • Known easements or utility rights-of-way

2 Demand profile

  • • Number of people living on the land
  • • Planned livestock (species and approximate numbers)
  • • Garden / crop irrigation needs
  • • Buildings that will need water and power
  • • Desired level of independence (grid-tied, hybrid, full off-grid)
  • • Seasonal use vs year-round residence

3 Site constraints

  • • Soil type and percolation for septic
  • • Topography and drainage patterns
  • • Frost depth and freeze risk for pipes
  • • Solar exposure and shading
  • • Wind resource if considering wind power
  • • Local regulations and permitting difficulty

4 Budget & timeline

  • • Capital available for infrastructure
  • • Preference for phased vs all-at-once build
  • • DIY capacity vs hired work
  • • Target date for basic water and power online

Outputs

What Tractform calculates so you can plan water and power with confidence.

Water demand estimate

Daily and peak water needs for household, livestock, and irrigation. Comparison against known well yield or surface sources.

Water system options

Recommended approach (well, spring development, rainwater, hauled, or hybrid). Storage sizing, pump requirements, and basic distribution layout.

Power demand estimate

Baseline and peak electrical loads for the planned buildings, tools, pumps, and lifestyle. Seasonal variation if relevant.

Power system options

Grid extension cost vs solar + battery vs generator hybrid. Rough system size, panel count, and battery capacity for off-grid or hybrid goals.

Septic & wastewater

System type suitability based on soil and household size. Estimated cost range and permitting notes.

Phased build plan

Ordered sequence of work with rough cost and time for each major step. What must be done first so later stages (buildings, animals, garden) can proceed.

True infrastructure cost

Combined capital cost for water, power, and septic to reach a basic livable standard. Ongoing operating cost estimates (fuel, maintenance, utility bills).

Risk & dependency flags

Single points of failure, drought vulnerability, grid outage exposure, and regulatory hurdles called out in plain language.
