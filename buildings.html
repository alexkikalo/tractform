Stage 3 of the path

Buildings & Roads

Shelter, storage, workshops, and the paths that connect them. This stage sizes what you need, where it should sit, and in what order to build so the land becomes usable without wasted effort.

The question this stage answers

“What structures and access does this land need to support daily life, animals, and work — and how should they be sized, placed, and sequenced?”

Inputs

What you tell Tractform about the people, animals, and work the buildings must serve.

1 Household & use

  • • Number of people living on the land (now and planned)
  • • Full-time residence vs seasonal / weekend use
  • • Work done on site (shop, office, processing, guests)
  • • Existing structures and their condition

2 Animals & production

  • • Livestock types and approximate numbers
  • • Need for coops, barns, loafing sheds, milking parlor
  • • Feed and equipment storage requirements
  • • Garden / crop processing and cold storage needs

3 Site & access

  • • Current road and driveway condition
  • • Distance from public road to main building sites
  • • Terrain, drainage, and frost heave risk
  • • Preferred building locations and solar orientation
  • • Equipment that must access the site (truck, tractor, trailer)

4 Constraints & preferences

  • • Budget for structures and access
  • • DIY vs hired construction capacity
  • • Material preferences (pole barn, stick, metal, timber frame)
  • • Local code, setback, and permit requirements
  • • Phasing preference (shelter first vs production first)

Outputs

What Tractform calculates so you can size, place, and sequence buildings and roads correctly.

Structure list & sizing

Recommended buildings (house or cabin, barn, coop, workshop, storage, root cellar) with approximate footprint and capacity based on household and livestock numbers.

Placement guidance

Suggested locations relative to water, power, wind, solar, drainage, and daily movement patterns. Separation needs for animals, feed, and living space.

Access & roads

Driveway and internal path requirements. Width, grade, and surface recommendations for year-round access by truck, tractor, or emergency vehicles.

Build sequence

Ordered plan for what to build first so the land becomes usable quickly. Temporary vs permanent options and when each structure becomes critical.

Cost ranges

Rough capital cost for each major structure and for access work. DIY vs contractor ranges and notes on where cost commonly overruns.

Code & permit flags

Likely permit triggers, setback issues, and agricultural exemptions that may apply. What to verify with the county before breaking ground.

Integration with earlier stages

How building locations and sizes interact with water, power, and septic already planned. Conflicts or efficiencies called out clearly.

Readiness for production

What must be standing before animals arrive or serious gardening begins. Minimum viable shelter and storage package.
