Structures & how they’re built

Select a topic to see sizing, foundation options, and practical construction notes.

Foundations

Match foundation type to soil, frost, and structure weight — not to what looks cheapest on paper.

Slab on grade

Common for shops and some houses where frost heave is managed. Needs good drainage and proper prep under the concrete.

Pier / post

Suited to pole barns, small cabins, and uneven ground. Posts below frost line; bracing and soil bearing matter.

Stem wall / crawl

Raises the floor, allows access to plumbing. More material and forming work; good in wet or sloping sites.

What to decide first

Frost depth, soil type, structure load, and whether you need a workshop floor or animal bedding area. Local code may dictate minimums.

Chicken coop

Predator-proof, dry, ventilated — size to the flock you actually keep.

Space

About 4 sq ft inside per bird; 8–10+ sq ft in the run. Overcrowding drives stress and disease.

Structure

Hardware cloth (not chicken wire) on openings. Secure latches. Roosts and nest boxes sized to the flock.

Floor & bedding

Solid floor easier to clean; deep litter works if managed. Keep bedding dry.

Placement

Morning sun, drainage, and easy access for daily chores. Away from low wet spots.

Barn / shed

Pole barn and simple sheds cover most homestead needs when sized and sited well.

Pole barn

Fast, economical clear span. Posts in ground or on piers; metal or wood skin. Plan door height for equipment.

Sizing

Start from what you store and shelter — tractor, hay, livestock, shop — not from a round number of square feet.

Orientation

Open side away from prevailing winter wind when possible. Roof runoff managed so it doesn’t create mud at doors.

Sequence

Often worth building a modest shed first for tools and feed, then a larger barn when the pattern of use is clear.

Roads & access

Year-round access for truck, tractor, and emergency vehicles shapes everything else.

Driveway

Width, grade, and base material. Steep or soft ground fails in mud season without proper build-up.

Internal paths

Routes between house, barn, coops, and garden that stay walkable and workable in wet weather.

Gates & turnarounds

Space to turn a trailer without destroying pasture. Gate placement that matches daily chore flow.

Drainage

Crown, ditches, or culverts so the road doesn’t become a creek. Water management is road longevity.
