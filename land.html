Land

Evaluate ground with clear eyes

Guidance for searching and judging parcels — not a listing search. Choose a focus below to see what matters.

What to look for

Turn goals into a short search brief you can take to listings or a realtor.

Climate fit

Hardiness zone, frost dates, and rainfall set what you can grow and stock. Set your zone in the bar above so other pages can use it.

Distance

Max drive to work, family, or town is a lifestyle constraint. Long drives cut time on the land and raise true cost.

Must-haves

Well, septic, power at the road, legal access, usable open ground — list what you will not compromise on.

Nice-to-haves

Timber, pond, existing barn, south slope, privacy. Score these so you can compare parcels honestly.

Carrying capacity

Rough sense of what acreage and climate can support before you commit.

Livestock

Stocking rates depend on rainfall and forage quality. Use regional rules of thumb, then cut them if the ground is poor or drought-prone.

Gardens

Open, sunny, workable soil near water matters more than total acreage. A quarter-acre of good ground can outproduce five acres of shade and clay.

Timber & fuel

Wooded land can supply firewood and posts if managed. It does not automatically mean pasture or garden without clearing cost and time.

Water limits

Well yield, surface water rights, and drought history often cap animals and irrigation harder than acreage does.

Parcel checklist

Questions to ask and flags to notice when you walk a property.

• Legal access and easements recorded?

• Water source, yield, and quality tested or documented?

• Septic permitted and functional — or soil suitable for one?

• Power at the property or distance and cost to extend?

• Flood, fire, and wind exposure?

• Soil: depth, drainage, rock, contamination history?

• Fencing condition and predator pressure?

• Restrictions: HOA, ag exemptions, building setbacks?

After you buy

Order of work so the land becomes usable without wasting capital.

1. Access & boundaries

Driveway, gates, survey awareness. Know where you can legally work and drive.

2. Water & power

Secure reliable water and a basic power plan before animals or serious building.

3. Shelter & storage

Minimum dry space for people, tools, and feed. Temporary is fine if permanent comes next.

4. Production

Only then stock animals or expand gardens at a scale the household can sustain.
