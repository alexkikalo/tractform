Stage 1 of the path

From no land to owning land

The first step is choosing the right ground. Tractform helps you evaluate parcels, estimate what the land can realistically support, and understand the true cost of bringing it to life.

The question this stage answers

“Can this piece of land actually support the life I want to live on it — and what will it take to get there?”

Inputs

What you tell Tractform about your situation and preferences.

1 Location & constraints

  • • Preferred regions / states / counties
  • • Maximum distance from work, family, or town
  • • Hard climate limits (zone, freeze dates, rainfall)
  • • Must-haves vs nice-to-haves (well, septic, power, road access, timber, pasture)

2 Household

  • • Number of people
  • • Primary goals (food, livestock, timber, privacy, off-grid, income)
  • • Skill level and available labor hours
  • • Capital available for purchase + initial setup

3 Land preferences

  • • Target acreage range
  • • Terrain (flat, rolling, steep, mixed)
  • • Existing improvements (house, barn, fencing, well, septic)
  • • Soil type preferences or deal-breakers
  • • Water sources (well, spring, creek, pond, municipal)

4 Risk tolerance

  • • Flood, wildfire, drought, wind, frost pocket tolerance
  • • Willingness to do heavy clearing or earthwork
  • • Timeline (need land this year vs 2–5 years)

Outputs

What Tractform calculates and shows so you can decide with clarity.

Suitability score

How well a specific parcel matches your goals and constraints. Clear flags for deal-breakers (no water rights, severe flood zone, impossible access).

Carrying capacity estimates

Rough livestock capacity based on acreage, rainfall, and forage. Garden and crop potential based on soil, zone, and available open land. Timber or firewood potential if wooded.

Infrastructure readiness

What already exists versus what must be built. Estimated cost and complexity for well, septic, power, driveway, and fencing.

True cost of ownership

Purchase price + closing + initial infrastructure + first-year operating costs. Ongoing property tax and insurance estimates.

Preparation path

Ordered list of what must be done after purchase before animals or serious gardening can begin. Time and capital required for each major step.

Risk summary

Climate, water, access, legal (easements, HOA, agricultural restrictions), and soil risks in plain language.
