Stage 5 of the path

Family Needs

Ongoing capacity. This stage keeps the whole plan matched to the people living it — labor, food, time, money, and realistic limits — so the land serves the household instead of the other way around.

The question this stage answers

“Can the people on this land actually sustain the systems we planned — and what does a workable, livable year look like for this household?”

Inputs

What you tell Tractform about the household that must carry the plan.

1 Household profile

  • • Number of adults and children
  • • Ages and physical capacity for heavy work
  • • Off-farm work hours and commute
  • • Experience with animals, gardening, building, and systems

2 Food & lifestyle goals

  • • Target share of food from the land
  • • Dietary preferences and hard constraints
  • • Interest in preservation (canning, freezing, curing)
  • • Desire for surplus, gifts, or small sales

3 Time & labor

  • • Available hours per week for chores and projects
  • • Seasonal flexibility (more time in summer vs fixed schedule)
  • • Help from family, neighbors, or hired hands
  • • Tolerance for early mornings, evening chores, and 7-day weeks

4 Money & resilience

  • • Off-farm income that must be protected
  • • Capital and operating budget for the homestead
  • • Emergency fund and risk tolerance
  • • Long-term goals (debt-free, full-time on land, part-time forever)

Outputs

What Tractform calculates so the plan stays human-scale and sustainable.

Labor reality check

Total weekly and seasonal hours required by the full plan versus hours the household can actually give. Clear over-commitment flags.

Food balance

Estimated calories, protein, and key foods the production systems can supply. Gap between goal and realistic output in year one and at maturity.

Cash flow sketch

Rough annual operating costs (feed, fuel, seed, repairs, taxes, insurance) against off-farm income and any planned sales.

Capacity limits

Maximum animals, garden area, and projects the household can sustain without burnout. Suggested ceilings for the current life stage.

Rhythm of the year

High-load seasons and recovery windows. When the plan demands the most and where rest or hired help is non-negotiable.

Dependency map

Which systems fail if one person is sick, traveling, or leaves. Single points of human failure called out plainly.

Adjustment recommendations

Specific places to scale back production, delay buildings, or simplify infrastructure so the plan fits the people.

Path to more

What can grow later when skills, time, or capital increase. A clear order for expanding without overloading the household.
