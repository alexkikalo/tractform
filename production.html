Stage 4 of the path

Production

Animals or garden — or both. This stage sizes the living systems that feed the household so they match the land, the labor available, and the buildings already planned.

The question this stage answers

“What combination of animals and gardens can this land and this household realistically support — and what will it take to keep them productive?”

Inputs

What you tell Tractform about goals, land capacity, and available labor.

1 Goals & priorities

  • • Primary aim (food for household, surplus for sale, breeding stock, self-reliance)
  • • Preference for livestock, gardens, or both
  • • Species or crops of interest (and hard no’s)
  • • Target share of household food from the land

2 Land capacity

  • • Open / pasture acreage and quality
  • • Wooded or marginal land that can support animals or forage
  • • Garden-ready ground (soil, sun, water access)
  • • Climate zone, frost dates, and rainfall
  • • Existing fencing, coops, barns, or beds

3 Household capacity

  • • Available labor hours per week
  • • Skill level with animals and gardening
  • • Number of people who will do daily chores
  • • Willingness to process meat, milk, eggs, or harvest
  • • Time away from the land (travel, off-farm work)

4 Constraints

  • • Budget for stock, seed, feed, fencing, and tools
  • • Local regulations (animal limits, slaughter, sales)
  • • Predator and pest pressure
  • • Water available for livestock and irrigation
  • • Preference for low-input vs intensive systems

Outputs

What Tractform calculates so production stays matched to land and labor.

Livestock package

Recommended species and numbers based on acreage, forage, labor, and goals. Starter flock or herd sizes that stay manageable in year one.

Garden & crop plan

Bed or field area needed for target yields. Crop mix suited to the zone and household diet. Succession and season-extension notes.

Feed & inputs

Estimated annual feed, seed, amendments, and bedding. How much can be grown on-site versus purchased.

Labor load

Daily, weekly, and seasonal chore estimates. Flags when the plan exceeds available hours or skill.

Yield & food contribution

Realistic output in eggs, meat, milk, vegetables, or calories. Share of household food needs the plan can cover.

Infrastructure dependencies

Shelter, fencing, water, and storage that must already exist (or be built next) before animals or serious planting begin.

Phased start

Year-one starter package versus full target. What to add only after the first season succeeds.

Risk flags

Overstocking, drought feed gaps, predator exposure, labor bottlenecks, and regulatory limits called out in plain language.
