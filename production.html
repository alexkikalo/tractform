Livestock

Choose an animal

Select a type to see land, water, feed, shelter, lifespan, and care requirements.

Sheep

Meat, wool, or dual-purpose. Manageable for small acreage when stocking rates and fencing are right.

Land

Roughly 3–5 sheep per acre on decent pasture (less on poor forage). Rotate paddocks to protect ground and reduce parasites.

Water

About 1–2 gallons per sheep per day; more in heat or for lactating ewes. Clean, reliable access is non-negotiable.

Feed

Pasture first. Hay in winter or drought. Mineral mix formulated for sheep (avoid cattle mixes high in copper).

Shelter

Windbreak or simple three-sided shed is often enough. Dry ground and protection from driving rain matter more than a full barn.

Lifespan & cycle

Typically 10–12 years. Breeding ewes productive ~5–7 years. Lambs reach market weight in months depending on breed and feed.

Care rhythm

Daily check and water. Hoof trimming, shearing (wool breeds), and parasite management on a seasonal schedule. Guardian animals or solid fencing for predators.

Chickens

Eggs, meat, or both. Lowest barrier to entry for most households; still need solid predator protection and daily care.

Land / space

Minimum ~4 sq ft inside coop per bird, 8–10+ sq ft run. Free-range needs secure fencing or supervision.

Water

About 1 pint per bird per day; more in heat. Waterers must stay clean and unfrozen.

Feed

Layer or grower ration as appropriate. Scratch and kitchen scraps as supplement, not the base. Grit if not on open ground.

Shelter

Predator-proof coop with roosts and nest boxes. Ventilation without drafts. Secure run or mobile tractor.

Lifespan & cycle

Layers productive ~2–4 years; lifespan longer. Meat birds processed at weeks to a few months depending on type.

Care rhythm

Daily feed, water, egg collection, quick health check. Bedding changes and deep cleans on a weekly/seasonal rhythm.

Goats

Milk, meat, or brush clearing. Excellent escape artists — fencing quality matters as much as feed.

Land

Often 4–6 goats per acre depending on browse. Prefer browse and brush over pure grass. Rotate to protect plants and soil.

Water

1–3 gallons per day depending on size, heat, and lactation. Fresh water preferred; they can be picky.

Feed

Browse, hay, and minerals. Dairy animals need higher-quality forage and often grain. Copper needs differ from sheep.

Shelter

Dry, draft-free shelter. They dislike rain more than cold. Strong fencing (woven wire, electric) is essential.

Lifespan & cycle

Often 10–15 years. Does productive for several years of kidding. Meat kids finished in months.

Care rhythm

Daily feed and check. Hooves, parasites, and (for dairy) consistent milking schedule. Social animals — plan for at least two.

Cattle

Beef or dairy. Higher land and capital needs than small stock; stocking rate and winter feed dominate the plan.

Land

Often 1–2 acres per animal on good pasture (varies widely by rainfall and forage). Winter feed may require more land or purchased hay.

Water

10–20+ gallons per day per animal. Reliable source and tank or trough capacity matter year-round.

Feed

Pasture in season; hay and possibly supplement in winter. Mineral program matched to region and forage tests.

Shelter

Windbreaks and dry lying areas often enough in mild climates. Working pens and solid fencing required for handling.

Lifespan & cycle

Beef finished in 18–30 months depending on system. Breeding cows productive for several years. Dairy is a daily commitment for years.

Care rhythm

Daily checks. Handling facilities for safe work. Veterinary and breeding calendar. Labor scales quickly with herd size.

Pigs

Efficient meat on small acreage. Strong fencing, wallows or shade, and a clear finishing plan are essential.

Land / space

Pastured systems need rotation to avoid mud and root damage. Lots or paddocks with strong perimeter fencing.

Water

Several gallons per day plus water for cooling in heat. They will tip weak waterers — design for it.

Feed

Commercial ration or carefully balanced farm mix. Kitchen and garden waste only as a supplement, not the whole diet.

Shelter

Shade and a dry sleeping area. Simple A-frames or huts work. Mud management in wet climates is a real design problem.

Lifespan & cycle

Meat pigs typically finished in 5–8 months. Breeding stock kept longer. Plan processing before you need it.

Care rhythm

Daily feed and check. Fencing inspections. Move paddocks if rotational. Respect their strength and intelligence when handling.
